***Iceland, Emerging Frontier of Innovation:  Cutting edge Multidisciplinary Research, Tourist Destination and Northern Sea Route—'China’s Arctic Silk Road’***

**Last week from October 5th to October 11th 2026 I had an amazing journey and a life time opportunity visiting Iceland. I was invited by the University of Iceland, Arctic Council and more importantly courtesy Professor Jon Geir Petursson, School of Engineering and Natural Sciences, Environment and Natural Resources Graduate Program, Reykjavik. Since in the previous century I had done my Masters in Geography and have remained zealous about political geography, so I was aware of Iceland’s strategic significance and why Greenland ‘grab’ was in headlines in recent months but my family was skeptical and thought it was some remote wilderness in the ice capped polar region and I being a senior citizen, it would be an arduous journey for me. However, it was a incredibly smooth journey, from Lahore to Doha about 3 hours, from Doha to Oslo 6 and half hours and from Oslo to Reykjavik another less than 3 hours and by dinner time on the same day, I was in Reykjavik. And there was Ahmed to receive me; such gestures in life make you passionate and proud of your students. I cannot thank him and his wife enough for personal attention, company, care and hospitality.   The University of Iceland was a generous host for my entire stay and travel an unusual honour and for me it’s hard to find words of gratitude for Professor Petursson, the University and its Rector. Travel back was equally smooth from Reykjavik to Helsinki, 3 and half hours and from there to Doha 6 and half hours and back to Lahore. Finn Air offered free, only water, during the three and half hour flight but I found this phrase on the paper cup inspiring, testy and oozing positivity; “We Finns always believe that better days are ahead.”** 

 **Iceland is serene, scenic, clean and the volcanic eruptions are a common occurrence and signs of dark ash and lava laden plateau like charred humps are visible along with snow clad mountains The country has a population of about 400,000 and I was told almost 50% live in and around the capital city Reykjavik. In the past five years the number of Pakistanis has risen from a few hundred to an estimated 3,000 to date and there is a Pakistani restaurant in the heart of downtown (more later on this). I enjoyed the heavenly fresh and delicious Icelandic Cod, Salman, Tuna and organic Icelandic lamb dishes. Temperatures’ during the week were between 5 to 7 Centigrade and had two sunny days, which was nice. It was the end of tourist season, so plenty of Chinese, Japanese and Korean, plus American and European visitors were visible, downtown is relatively small, lively and throbbing. On a nice day, the cultural heritage sites, museums and eateries are at a walkable distance. Reykjavik is one of the most expensive cities in the world and low and behold; Iceland has American bases but does not have any McDonalds!!** 

**Arctic Assembly sessions were divided into two parts, the Plenary Sessions, in which the key statesmen from across the world , the presidents. prime ministers, foreign ministers/ cabinet ministers, Commanders of various forces delivered policy speeches followed by a brief Q/A session. In the second part, researchers, policy analysts, academics, business leaders and senior government officials shared their perspectives. Participating in the Arctic Assembly sessions has been an incredible experience in learning and witnessing presentations on cutting edge research topics, data driven and futuristic, in a multidisciplinary and global context—ranging from Arctic Ice melt to Third Pole glaciers, dynamics of geopolitics to bio-diversity, preserving indigenous communities, cultures, protecting and adapting livelihoods to sustainable development.**

**I was in Iceland as I mentioned above; I have the honour to be on the doctoral supervisory committee of Ahmed Nawaz, a brilliant doctoral student at the University of Iceland, who did his Executive Masters at the Centre for Public Policy & Governance, FCCU and another one at the University of Iceland. Ahmed has a innovative research design, theoretically rigorous, laden with data driven methodology on rarely studied Protected Areas(PA) of Northern Areas of Pakistan, Professor Petursson being the principal supervisor .  The doctoral committee comprising of Professor Petursson, me and Professor Thomas Barry, Dean, School of Humanities and Social Sciences, University of Akureyri, Iceland. For over a year the committee has been meeting on Zoom, it met for the first time in person on October 6, 2026 at the University Iceland to ‘review research design and publications’ of the doctoral fellow.**

 **I was also invited by the Institute of International Affairs at the university to be the keynote speaker and on 7th, I gave my keynote address on the broad theme of; The Interconnectedness of the global glacier regions: Perspectives from the HKH Karakoram glacier landscapes in N- Pakistan, which was followed by a panel discussion. The session  was moderated by Professor Petrusson, who gave an excellent presentation along with maps on the topography and socio- cultural life of the people of HKH region. I was humbled and honored that session was attended by Professor Madam Silja Bara Omarsdottir, Rector University of Iceland and a leading Political Science, IR Scholar, who gave her concluding remarks and thanked the participants for a lively and engaging discussion.**
